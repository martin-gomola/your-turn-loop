## Handoff

End every final answer with a short handoff block, so the user can see at a glance what happened and whether anything waits on them. Tools such as the Your Turn inbox parse it, so keep the exact format: each field on its own line, a blank line between fields, plain Markdown outside code blocks, at the very end of the reply. Questions and pauses for review get the block too; progress updates do not.

**Outcome:** The importer skips malformed rows instead of aborting the file.

**Next step:** review: confirm the skipped-row log format in importer.py

- Outcome: one sentence under 160 characters with the concrete result, finding or blocker. Include a limit when it changes the meaning.
- Next step: `none` when nothing waits on the user. Otherwise one of `answer:`, `decide:`, `review:`, `approve:` or `run:`, then the single action they must take, under 120 characters. Finish the work you are allowed to do first. Optional suggestions go in the body, not here.
- From the second turn on, add `**Recap:**` with one or two sentences about the whole chat: the goal, what is settled, what is open.
- When you settle a trade-off this turn, add `**Decision:** <question> -> <chosen> (over <alternatives>). <reason>`.
- Keep labels and prefixes in English and write the rest in the reply's language. Do not use these labels anywhere else in the answer.

Format: Handoff protocol 1.0, https://martin-gomola.github.io/your-turn-loop/spec.html

01Fields

Outcome required

One sentence under 160 characters: the concrete result, recommendation, finding or blocker. A limit that changes the meaning of the result belongs here, for example “phone layout is unverified”. A reader may cut it at 200 characters.

Next step required

Exactly one action under 120 characters, or none. It starts with one of the prefixes below and names what the user must do and where. A reader may cut it at 200 characters.

Recap from the second turn

One or two sentences under 400 characters covering the whole chat, not just this turn: the goal, what is settled and why, and what is still open.

Decision zero or more

A consequential choice actually made this turn, one per line:

**Decision:** <question> -> <chosen> (over <alternatives>). <reason>

Separate alternatives with commas. (over …) is optional. A choice still waiting on the user belongs in Next step as decide:, never here.

02Next step

Any value other than none means the chat needs the user. A writer must finish the authorized work it can do first, then pick the value by the real human dependency:

ValueUse when
noneNothing required waits on the user. Delivered advice and findings with a stated limit fit here too. Optional suggestions go in the body.
answer: …Required information is missing and only the user can supply it.
decide: …A choice belongs to the user. Name at least two options; put the recommendation in the body.
review: …A prepared artifact waits for the user's judgment. A finished result or uncertainty alone is not a review.
approve: …A necessary action needs permission the user has not already given.
run: …The next command belongs to the user. Give the exact command, not just “deploy”.

03Layout

  • A writer must include Outcome and Next step in every final answer, including questions and pauses for review. Progress updates need no block.
  • The fields form one group at the very top or, preferably, the very bottom, with no prose between them. With the block at the bottom, the answer ends there.
  • Each field is one line of plain Markdown outside code blocks, with a blank line between fields, because chat apps join adjacent lines into one paragraph.
  • Labels, value prefixes, none, -> and (over …) stay in English. The prose is in the language of the reply.
  • A writer should not use the field labels anywhere else in the answer, so that no other line is mistaken for a field.

04Reading the block

Writers emit the canonical form above. Readers should also accept the variants agents produce in practice:

  • Labels in any case, bold or plain, after a heading mark, quote marker or list bullet.
  • Label aliases: Result for Outcome; Next, Your next step and Ďalší krok for Next step.
  • nothing, no action, n/a or a dash as none. A next step with no known prefix is still an action for the user.
  • → as well as -> in a decision, and or as well as commas between alternatives.

Readers must ignore lines inside fenced code blocks, so an example in the body never overrides the real handoff. An answer without an Outcome line is unformatted; a reader may fall back to its own summary, but should mark it as inferred.

06Implementations

  • Writer: the your-turn-handoff skill teaches Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Antigravity and Gemini CLI to end answers with the block. Install it alone or with the cross-agent loop.
  • Reader: the Your Turn inbox turns the block into one card per chat and lists every chat whose next step is not none under Needs you.

Version 1.0 describes the format used by your-turn-handoff 0.7.0 and later. A change that breaks existing readers or writers raises the major version.
